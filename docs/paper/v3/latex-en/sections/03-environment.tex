\section{Experimental Environment}

\subsection{A Literature-Calibrated Synthetic Environment}
The performance of the proposed reallocation architecture is verified
using numerical experiments. In order to conduct the experiments in a
more realistic setup, scales and operating ranges reported in existing
container terminal studies are employed to the extent possible. The
environment is therefore not a replica of the operating data of a
particular terminal but a \emph{literature-calibrated synthetic
environment}. Table~\ref{tab:env} separates the values taken from prior
work from the values this study fixed within those ranges.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Environment components: basis in prior work and the value used here}
\label{tab:env}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Component & Basis in prior work & Value used here \\
\midrule
Multiple blocks & 16 and 20 blocks~\cite{ref15,ref16} & 21 blocks \\
Multiple yard cranes & Several cranes per block~\cite{ref8,ref17} & 2 per block \\
Long-run discrete event & Multi-week continuous runs~\cite{ref9} & 30 days, state carried \\
Time-of-day arrivals & Hourly probability, turn time~\cite{ref13,ref14} & Base plus three peaks \\
Vessel class, call size & Three classes, differing calls~\cite{ref16,ref23} & Small/medium/large \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The 21 blocks, the exact demand levels, and the arrival-curve
coefficients are therefore design values of this study. The citations
establish that the chosen scale and variation lie within the modelling
range of existing work; they do not indicate operating data from a
specific port.

\subsection{Terminal and Time Settings}
The yard has 21 blocks with two yard cranes each, 42 in total. Block
capacity is 1{,}440 slots with an initial occupancy of 65\%. The layout
is one-dimensional and horizontal, with the gate and the quay on
opposite sides. The policy decision interval is 60\,s. Operation runs
for 30 continuous days, and the middle 28 days are compared after
excluding the first and last day. The crane work sequence gives priority
to actual service work and, within it, to the job with the shortest
expected processing time.

A day is not a point at which the terminal stops but a cut at which cost
and intermediate results are separated. Yard inventory, queues, crane
state, and unfinished jobs carry over to the next day. The first day
connects the initial state to the continuous flow, and the last day
preserves the residual effect of preceding work.

\subsection{External Truck Demand and the Arrival Process}
The daily demand $D_d$ of day $d$ was sampled from a discrete
distribution over $\{3{,}500,\,5{,}000,\,7{,}500,\,12{,}500,\,15{,}000\}$
trucks with probabilities $\{0.30,\,0.30,\,0.25,\,0.10,\,0.05\}$. The
first three levels represent smooth, normal, and high demand; the last
two represent congested and heavily congested conditions. Within a day,
the announcement time of each job was drawn from the time-of-day arrival
density $f(t)$, which is a mixture of a uniform base over 24 hours and
normal peaks:

\begin{equation}
f(t)=0.38\,U(0,24)+0.62\sum_{m=1}^{3}w_m
\mathcal N_{[0,24)}(t;\mu_m,\sigma_m^2),\quad
(\mu_m,\sigma_m,w_m)\in\{(10,1.5,.317),(15,2.5,.633),(21,1,.05)\}.
\end{equation}

Time is measured in hours and the truncated density is renormalised so
that it integrates to one over a day. The time-of-day arrival model and
its effect on turn time follow~\cite{ref13,ref14}; the mixture weights
and peak centres were selected as design values so that congestion
sensitivity can be compared across demand levels, and are not claimed to
be estimates fitted to a particular terminal. The uniform base keeps
arrivals non-zero at night, so the yard state carried
across the day boundary is not an artefact of an empty night.

\subsection{Vessels and Quay Operations}
Vessels were divided into three classes---small (50{,}000\,GT,
3{,}000\,TEU, two quay cranes), medium (100{,}000\,GT, 7{,}500\,TEU,
four), and large (150{,}000\,GT, 14{,}000\,TEU, six)---with idle costs of
KRW 149{,}500, 299{,}000, and 448{,}500 per hour respectively. Here, the
parameters were generated as follows. The call volume of a vessel of
capacity $C$ TEU was sampled as $C\cdot u$ with $u\sim U[0.15,0.30]$. The daily composition was two small vessels and
one medium vessel, to which one large vessel was added with probability
0.30. The throughput of one quay crane was set to 27.5\,move/h, which
lies within the 24, 30, and 36\,move/h range used in the
literature~\cite{ref16}. Blocking across quay cranes was summed and
divided by the number assigned, giving the idle time per vessel.

\subsection{Units of Yard Crane Capacity}
The reference service time of one crane is 180\,s per move, so the
nominal total move capacity is

\begin{equation}
\mu_{YC}=21\times2\times\frac{3600}{180}=840\ \text{move/h}.
\end{equation}

A move is a container movement and is not a truck. One truck job may
require several moves and rehandles, and the realised service time
varies with position and job type. We follow the guidance that equipment
capacity must be reported together with move type, automation delay, and
handling-vehicle conditions~\cite{ref18}.

\subsection{Cost Function}
The total cost over the interval $[d,d+1)$ is the sum of four terms:

\begin{equation}
\Phi_d=C_{wait,d}+C_{move,d}+C_{rehandle,d}+C_{vessel,d}.
\end{equation}

Truck dwell costs KRW 40{,}000 per hour, with the same rate applied
again to any excess beyond one hour; the safe trucking freight rate is
used as the monetary anchor and the hourly conversion is a study
value~\cite{ref24}. Additional crane movement costs KRW 60{,}000 per
hour of extra work and a rehandle costs KRW 2{,}000, both design values.
Vessel idling costs KRW 2.99 per GT-hour, converted from the excess
berthing rate of KRW 29.9 per 10\,GT-hour~\cite{ref25}.

With $h_i$ the terminal dwell time of truck $i$, $m$ the additional
crane working time, $R$ the number of rehandles, and $T_v$ the
policy-related idle time of vessel $v$:

\begin{equation}
C_{wait}=\sum_i 40{,}000\{h_i+\max(0,h_i-1)\},\ \
C_{move}=60{,}000\,m,\ \ C_{rehandle}=2{,}000\,R,\ \
C_{vessel}=\sum_v 2.99\,GT_v T_v.
\end{equation}

Structural vessel time that the reallocation policy cannot change, such
as berthing and quarantine, is separated as a diagnostic value. All four
terms are computed as differences of cumulative values at the same day
boundary.
