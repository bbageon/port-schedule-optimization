\section{Policies and Learning}

\subsection{Notation and the Decision Unit}
Let the decision interval be $\delta=60$\,s, the set of yard blocks
$\mathcal B$, and the set of arrival-deferral candidates $\mathcal D$.
The current assignment of job $i$ is $z_i=(b_i,\tau_i)$, where $b_i$ is
the block and $\tau_i$ the announced expected arrival time. A
reallocation action is

\begin{equation}
a_i=(b'_i,\Delta_i)\in\mathcal A_i(t),\qquad
z_i(a_i)=(b'_i,\tau_i+\Delta_i).
\end{equation}

Keeping the original assignment is $a_i^0=(b_i,0)$; block reallocation
is $b'_i\ne b_i$; arrival-time adjustment is $\Delta_i>0$. Candidates
that change both are allowed. Outbound jobs are excluded from block
reallocation because the container to be retrieved has a fixed location.

With $n_i$ the announcement time, $g_i$ the gate-in time, and $r_i$ an
indicator of prior review, the set newly reviewed at time $t$ is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_t=\{i\mid n_i\le t<n_i+\delta,\ g_i>t,\ r_i=0\}.
\end{equation}

That is, a job that has not yet arrived is reviewed at exactly one time
point, when its announcement first enters the eligibility window.

\subsection{Independent Proposal and Acceptance Policies}
The proposal policy selects the action of lowest expected cost from its
own block state and the job and action features $o_i^P$:

\begin{equation}
\hat a_i=\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A_i(t)}
\hat c_{\theta}^{P}(o_i^P,a),\qquad
p_i=\mathbf 1[\hat a_i\ne a_i^0].
\end{equation}

If a proposal is made, the destination block or target time slot becomes
the accepting party. The acceptance policy compares the expected cost of
accepting and rejecting from the proposal message and its own state
$o_i^A$:

\begin{equation}
q_i=\mathbf 1\!\left[
\hat c_{\psi}^{A}(o_i^A,\hat a_i,\mathrm{accept})
\le
\hat c_{\psi}^{A}(o_i^A,\hat a_i,\mathrm{reject})
\right].
\end{equation}

If the remaining capacity of the target resource is zero, the request is
rejected before the network is consulted. The set of mutually agreed
candidates is $\mathcal J_t=\{i\in\mathcal E_t\mid p_iq_i=1\}$. The
problem structure in which two independent parties negotiate arrival
times from limited information follows~\cite{ref5,ref6}.

\subsection{Central Commitment Constraints}
The central procedure creates no new candidate and commits only within
$\mathcal J_t$. Agreed candidates are sorted by the predicted cost of
the acceptance policy in ascending order, with ties broken
lexicographically by job and coordinate. With $x_i\in\{0,1\}$ the
commitment indicator, $F_b(t)$ the free slots of the destination block,
and $K_k(t)$ the remaining quota of time slot $k$:

\begin{equation}
x_i\le p_iq_i,\qquad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}x_i\mathbf1[b'_i=b]\le F_b(t)
\quad(\forall b\in\mathcal B),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}x_i\mathbf1[k(i)=k]\le K_k(t)
\quad(\forall k),\qquad
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mathbf1[i\in\mathcal E_t]\le1\quad(\forall i).
\end{equation}

Block capacity is enforced in the present experiment. Where time-slot
quota data are unavailable we set $K_k(t)=\infty$ and interpret the
effect of that condition as the range of what time adjustment could
provide. All committed assignments are applied at the same instant, so a
job committed earlier does not change the state seen by later candidates
at the same decision point.

Algorithm~1 states both the consent condition---only candidates with a
proposal and an acceptance are committed---and the ownership condition
that each job is reviewed exactly once.

\subsection{Policy Models and Inputs}
Both policies are multilayer perceptrons that score fixed-length
features per candidate, and all blocks share one set of weights. The
proposal policy takes a 21-dimensional input and the acceptance policy a
16-dimensional one; both use two hidden layers of 64 units with ReLU.
The proposal policy takes the $\arg\min$ over all candidates and the
acceptance policy over accept and reject. Inputs comprise current
waiting, inventory, and remaining crane work, the public expected
arrival time, the route difference, and the announced volume around the
target time. Unrealised actual arrival and completion times, and the
simultaneous responses of other accepting parties, are excluded.

\subsection{Counterfactual Cost Labels}
For each sampled decision we clone the state and random stream
immediately before branching and run up to three worlds for three hours:

\begin{equation}
\phi_i^F=\Phi_H(\text{observed}),\quad
\phi_i^P=\Phi_H(\text{proposal reversed}),\quad
\phi_i^A=\Phi_H(\text{acceptance reversed}),\quad H=10{,}800\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{equation}

Decisions without an acceptance request produce only the proposal
comparison. For each policy the two actions are centred on their mean
and divided by KRW 100{,}000:

\begin{equation}
\bar\phi_i^P=\frac{\phi_i^F+\phi_i^P}{2},\qquad
y_{i,F}^P=\frac{\phi_i^F-\bar\phi_i^P}{100{,}000},\qquad
y_{i,P}^P=\frac{\phi_i^P-\bar\phi_i^P}{100{,}000}.
\end{equation}

The same centring is applied to $(\phi_i^F,\phi_i^A)$ for the acceptance
policy. Subtracting the mean removes the shared cost without changing
the order of the two actions. The baseline that reverses a single action
connects to difference rewards and multi-agent counterfactual
baselines~\cite{ref10,ref11}.

\subsection{Training Settings and Operational Separation}
The per-day sample cap is 64 decisions and the exploration probability
decreases linearly from 0.50 to 0.05. Training and validation are split
80/20 at the decision level. Optimisation uses Adam with a learning rate
of $3\times10^{-4}$, a Huber loss with $\beta=1.0$, minibatches of up to
256 rows, and $\max(50,\min(600,4\times\text{training rows}))$ updates,
with gradients clipped at 1.0.

Counterfactual branches run in parallel across CPU cores and the labels
generated on a given day are replaced after that day's update. At
evaluation the weights are frozen, exploration is disabled, and no
counterfactual branch is invoked. Fig.~\ref{fig:learning} shows the
training and validation losses together with the exploration
probability.
