\section{Policies and Learning}

\subsection{Notation, Actions, and Policies}
Let the decision interval be $\delta=60$\,s and let the current
assignment of job $i$ be $z_i=(b_i,\tau_i)$, with $b_i$ the block and
$\tau_i$ the announced arrival time. A reallocation action is
$a_i=(b'_i,\Delta_i)\in\mathcal A_i(t)$, giving
$z_i(a_i)=(b'_i,\tau_i+\Delta_i)$: keeping the original assignment is
$a_i^0=(b_i,0)$, block reallocation is $b'_i\ne b_i$, and arrival-time
adjustment is $\Delta_i>0$; candidates that change both are allowed.
Outbound jobs are excluded from block reallocation because the container
to be retrieved has a fixed location. With $n_i$ the announcement time,
$g_i$ the gate-in time, and $r_i$ an indicator of prior review, the set
newly reviewed at $t$ is
$\mathcal E_t=\{i\mid n_i\le t<n_i+\delta,\ g_i>t,\ r_i=0\}$, so a job
that has not yet arrived is reviewed at exactly one time point.
The proposal policy selects the action of lowest expected cost from its
own block state and the job and action features $o_i^P$:

\begin{equation}
\hat a_i=\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A_i(t)}
\hat c_{\theta}^{P}(o_i^P,a),\qquad
p_i=\mathbf 1[\hat a_i\ne a_i^0].
\end{equation}

If a proposal is made, the destination block or target time slot becomes
the accepting party. The acceptance policy compares the expected cost of
accepting and rejecting from the proposal message and its own state
$o_i^A$:

\begin{equation}
q_i=\mathbf 1\!\left[
\hat c_{\psi}^{A}(o_i^A,\hat a_i,\mathrm{accept})
\le
\hat c_{\psi}^{A}(o_i^A,\hat a_i,\mathrm{reject})
\right].
\end{equation}

If the remaining capacity of the target resource is zero, the request is
rejected before the network is consulted. The set of mutually agreed
candidates is $\mathcal J_t=\{i\in\mathcal E_t\mid p_iq_i=1\}$. The
problem structure in which two independent parties negotiate arrival
times from limited information follows~\cite{ref6}.

\subsection{Central Commitment Constraints}
The central procedure creates no new candidate and commits only within
$\mathcal J_t$, sorting agreed candidates by the predicted cost of the
acceptance policy and breaking ties lexicographically. With
$x_i\in\{0,1\}$ the commitment indicator, $F_b(t)$ the free slots of
block $b$, and $K_k(t)$ the remaining quota of slot $k$:

\begin{equation}
x_i\le p_iq_i,\quad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}\!x_i\mathbf1[b'_i\!=\!b]\le F_b(t),\quad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}\!x_i\mathbf1[k(i)\!=\!k]\le K_k(t),\quad
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\!\mathbf1[i\in\mathcal E_t]\le1.
\end{equation}

Block capacity is enforced here; where time-slot quota data are
unavailable we set $K_k(t)=\infty$ and read that condition as the range
of what time adjustment could provide. All committed assignments are
applied at the same instant, so a job committed earlier does not change
the state seen by later candidates. Two conditions therefore hold by
construction: only candidates carrying both a proposal and an acceptance
are committed, and each job is reviewed exactly once.

\subsection{Policy Models and Inputs}
Both policies are multilayer perceptrons that score fixed-length
features per candidate, with one set of weights shared across blocks:
21-dimensional input for the proposal policy and 16 for the acceptance
policy, two hidden layers of 64 units with ReLU, and 5{,}633 and 5{,}313
parameters respectively---fewer than 11{,}000 in total. Inputs comprise
current waiting, inventory, and remaining crane work, the public
expected arrival time, the route difference, and the announced volume
around the target time. Unrealised actual arrival and completion times,
and the simultaneous responses of other accepting parties, are
excluded.

\subsection{Counterfactual Cost Labels}
For each sampled decision we clone the state and random stream
immediately before branching and run up to three worlds for three hours:

\begin{equation}
\phi_i^F=\Phi_H(\text{observed}),
\quad \phi_i^P=\Phi_H(\text{proposal reversed}),
\quad \phi_i^A=\Phi_H(\text{acceptance reversed}),
\end{equation}

with $H=10{,}800$\,s. Decisions without an acceptance request produce only the proposal
comparison. For each policy the two actions are centred on their mean
$\bar\phi_i^P=(\phi_i^F+\phi_i^P)/2$ and divided by KRW 100{,}000,
giving targets $y_{i,F}^P=(\phi_i^F-\bar\phi_i^P)/10^5$ and
$y_{i,P}^P=(\phi_i^P-\bar\phi_i^P)/10^5$; the same centring is applied
to $(\phi_i^F,\phi_i^A)$ for the acceptance policy. Subtracting the mean
removes the shared cost without changing the order of the two actions.
The baseline that reverses a single action connects to difference
rewards and multi-agent counterfactual baselines~\cite{ref10,ref11}.

\subsection{Training Settings and Operational Separation}
The per-day sample cap is 64 decisions and the exploration probability
decreases linearly from 0.50 to 0.05, with an 80/20 train--validation
split at the decision level. Optimisation uses Adam
($3\times10^{-4}$), a Huber loss ($\beta=1.0$), minibatches of up to 256
rows, $\max(50,\min(600,4\times\text{rows}))$ updates, and gradients
clipped at 1.0. Counterfactual branches run in parallel across CPU cores
and each day's labels are replaced after that day's update. At
evaluation the weights are frozen, exploration is disabled, and no
counterfactual branch is invoked.

Training the pair took 4.7 hours of CPU time over 24 passes. At
operating time a decision costs one forward pass per candidate through a
network of the size given above, which is negligible beside the
constraint check it accompanies; the expense of the method lies entirely
in generating the counterfactual labels, and that work is offline and
never enters the operating path.
