\section{Conclusion}

A reinforcement learning architecture that jointly revisits the yard
block and arrival time of external truck jobs in response to real-time
congestion has been developed. The proposal and acceptance policies have
been modelled independently, and a central procedure applies the
assignments in one batch after checking mutual consent and resource
constraints. The uncertainty in the induced congestion effects has been
tackled with a counterfactual cost signal produced by discrete-event
simulations that differ in a single action, whereas at operating time
candidates are evaluated by the trained networks alone. The construction
connects truck arrival-time negotiation~\cite{ref5,ref6}, yard crane
operations~\cite{ref7,ref8}, and multi-agent counterfactual
learning~\cite{ref10,ref11} into one spatio-temporal assignment problem.

Three points follow. Spatial placement and temporal adjustment are
compared within one action range. The roles of proposing and accepting
are separated, extending the logic of distributed appointment
negotiation to a real-time reallocation policy~\cite{ref5,ref6}.
Counterfactual comparison makes the contribution of a single action
learnable from a joint cost, evaluating induced waiting and equipment
flow in the same monetary unit as the immediate movement cost; because
the branch computation is separated from operation, the decision path
remains a small cost model and a constraint check.

Observations in the literature-calibrated environment indicate that the
proposed structure carries spatio-temporal reallocation through to
execution, and that arrival-time adjustment and learned selection can
produce a direction of cost reduction under congested conditions. This
supports the view that real-time information at a smart port can lead to
assignment actions rather than monitoring alone~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3}.
The same direction held when the action range was matched, although on
the arrival-time axis alone the rule was lower on more days and the
proposed policy led only in total. Subsequent development can refine one
axis at a time: reflecting actual appointment quotas would make the
executable range of time adjustment concrete, repeating a wider variety
of congested conditions would delineate the contribution relative to the
untrained model, and where long-horizon effects matter a method that
redistributes reward over the whole decision sequence can be
compared~\cite{ref21}.

Observation, proposal, acceptance, commitment, execution, and
counterfactual learning have been organised into a single reproducible
structure. As automated equipment and appointment information become
more widespread, the proposed architecture can serve as a basis for
connecting real-time congestion information to explainable resource
assignment decisions.
