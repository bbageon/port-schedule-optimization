\section{Reproduction}

Reproduction separates the random seeds of training and evaluation and
lets all policies on a given evaluation day share the truck demand,
vessel schedule, and service random streams. Policy weights and the
comparison conditions are frozen before evaluation. The policy is
trained in a diagnostic seed band with per-day checkpoints stored; a
30-day run per policy is then executed with frozen weights under a seed
that does not overlap training; the middle 28 days are paired by date to
compute total cost, median difference, and the sign test; and finally
the block and time actions are restricted one at a time to decompose the
source of the cost change. Seven checks listed in Sect.~5.2 are
automated. Each day stores the four cost terms, the demand level, the
transaction types, the unfinished jobs, and the long waits, so that a
reproduction report can state which demand and which action produced the
result rather than a total alone. The source code, the per-policy raw
results, and the scripts that generate every figure are released with
the paper.

\section{Conclusion}

\subsection{Summary}
A reinforcement learning architecture that jointly revisits the yard
block and arrival time of external truck jobs in response to real-time
congestion at a container terminal has been developed. The reallocation
proposal policy and the acceptance policy have been modelled
independently, and a central commitment procedure applies the
assignments in one batch after checking mutual consent and resource
constraints. The uncertainty in the induced congestion effects has been
tackled with a counterfactual cost signal: during training, costs are
produced by discrete-event simulations that differ in a single action,
whereas at operating time candidates are evaluated by the trained
networks alone. The
construction connects truck arrival-time negotiation~\cite{ref5,ref6},
yard crane operations~\cite{ref7,ref8}, and multi-agent counterfactual
learning~\cite{ref10,ref11} into one spatio-temporal assignment problem.

\subsection{Academic Implications}
Three points follow. Spatial placement and temporal adjustment are
compared within one action range, so the policy can consider both where
congestion occurs and when it is expected. The roles of proposing and
accepting are separated, which extends the operating logic of
distributed appointment negotiation to a real-time reallocation
policy~\cite{ref5,ref6}. Counterfactual comparison makes the
contribution of a single action learnable from a joint cost, evaluating
the induced waiting and equipment flow in the same monetary unit as the
immediate movement cost; because the branch computation is separated
from operation, the decision path remains a small cost model and a
constraint check.

\subsection{Application and Future Directions}
Observations in the literature-calibrated continuous synthetic
environment indicate that the proposed structure carries spatio-temporal
reallocation through to execution, and that arrival-time adjustment and
learned selection can produce a direction of cost reduction under
congested conditions. This supports the view that real-time information
at a smart port can lead to assignment actions rather than monitoring
alone~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3}. The same direction held when the action
range was matched: arrival-time adjustment lowered cost significantly
even under a rule ($+3.41\%$), and the proposed policy using the same
actions was lower still (20/28, $p=0.0357$). On the arrival-time axis
alone, however, the rule was lower on more days and the proposed policy
led only in total, which suggests that the learned selection was
optimised \emph{not towards winning frequently but towards reducing cost
sharply on the expensive days}.

Subsequent development can refine one axis at a time. Reflecting actual
appointment quotas would make the executable range of time adjustment
concrete, and repeating a wider variety of congested conditions would
delineate the contribution relative to the untrained model. Where
long-horizon effects matter, a method that redistributes reward over the
whole decision sequence can also be compared~\cite{ref21}.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks}
Observation, proposal, acceptance, commitment, execution, and
counterfactual learning have been organised into a single reproducible
structure. The experimental results indicate that this structure can
develop into a reinforcement learning policy that adjusts port space and
time together. As automated equipment and appointment information become
more widespread, the proposed architecture can serve as a basis for
connecting real-time congestion information to explainable resource
assignment decisions.
